# Search intent, content and AI search

Use the product's audience, actual questions and conversion goal. Distinguish
observed queries or customer evidence from inferred demand. Before adding a page,
check whether an existing page already satisfies that intent. Group related
questions when one useful page can answer them; split when audiences, purpose or
required depth justify independent pages.

A brief should state the audience/problem, useful answer or experience, evidence,
intended action and relevant existing pages. Choose the structure and length that
help readers. State sources, dates and methods where factual claims need them.
Do not fabricate measurements, reviews, author expertise or firsthand experience.

Keep the content inventory and backlog with the product. Use available query and
conversion evidence to prioritize, but do not treat a query with no dedicated page
as proof that another page is needed. Refresh factual errors or changed source
data. Investigate traffic losses before rewriting or deleting; account for changes
in demand, availability, query mix and measurement.

Choose blog location, publishing cadence and metadata from the product's delivery
and audience requirements. A blog, FAQ, RSS feed or fixed link count is not a
universal launch requirement. Preserve valuable URLs and use redirects when
appropriate. Indexing submissions and external distribution are separate actions
within the user's scope.

## AI-search checks

For Google, the ordinary search foundations still apply. Google does not use
llms.txt for search visibility, requires no special AI markup, and does not require
content to be split into tiny pages. FAQ rich results are no longer shown in Google
Search. FAQs may still help readers; do not score FAQPage markup as mandatory.

For another platform, verify its current bot names and documented behavior before
changing access. Training, search and user-requested fetching have different
purposes. Decide permissions with the product owner; permission to train is not
automatically required by a search-visibility goal.

Treat observed AI citations as sampled observations, recording the question,
platform/mode, time and actual cited URL. Bot visits, model recognition and
citations are different observations. None alone proves a technique caused an
improvement or guarantees future answers.
